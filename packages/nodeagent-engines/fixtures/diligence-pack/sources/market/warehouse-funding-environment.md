# Warehouse Funding Environment

Prepared for: Startup Banking diligence team, Q3 2026 credit committee
Document type: funding market note (synthetic)
As of: July 31, 2026
Status: current

## Summary
- Warehouse facilities remain the main funding source for fintech small-business lenders.
- Terms tightened slightly in 2026 for lenders with rising loss rates.
- Figures below are synthetic ranges for this exercise.

## Typical terms (illustrative ranges)
- Advance rates on short-duration receivables: 85% to 90%
- Advance rates on term loans: 75% to 82%
- Spread over the benchmark rate for established originators: 350 to 450 basis points
- Spread for originators with loss-rate breaches: 500 to 650 basis points
- Typical tenor: two to three years with one-year extension options

## Common covenants
- Loss-rate triggers that stop new advances when annualized losses exceed a threshold.
- Obligor-concentration limits, often 10% to 20% of the eligible pool.
- Minimum liquidity covenants, often three to six months of operating expenses.
- Restricted cash reserves held by the lender and excluded from unrestricted cash.

## Renewal dynamics
- Lenders begin renewal talks nine to twelve months before maturity.
- Renewals in 2026 priced 50 to 100 basis points wider on average.
- Originators with short runway face tighter renewal terms.

## What to check in diligence
- Maturity dates and renewal status for each facility.
- Headroom to loss-rate triggers and concentration limits.
- Whether reported cash includes reserves held by the warehouse lender.
- Whether warehouse collection accounts are excluded from cash.

## Committee implications
- Treat any facility maturing within twelve months as a liquidity risk.
- Concentration near warehouse limits can force sudden deleveraging.
